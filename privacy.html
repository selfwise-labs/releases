What the apps store, where, and who can read it

This policy covers the three apps, the account service behind them and the connector for Claude and ChatGPT. It is written by the one person who runs them, Cristian Toader, and it says what actually happens.

Without an account, nothing you enter leaves your phone. The apps keep their data in a database on the device. The only connections they make are a check for updates at start.

With an account, your data is copied to a server so your phones stay in sync and, if you choose, so Claude or ChatGPT can read it. The server stores a username, a password hash, your app data and Sapor's meal photos. No email address, no name, no location, no advertising identifiers, no analytics.

Nothing is sold, shared or used for advertising. Only you, your assistant if you connect one, and Cristian as the operator can read your data. You can delete any of it.

Who runs this

Cristian Toader, publishing as Selfwise Labs, makes and operates the apps, the server and this page. There is no company behind them. The apps are in beta and used by people Cristian knows; accounts are created with an invite code he hands out. For any question or request about your data, write to him the way you got your invite code, or open an issue at github.com/selfwise-labs/releases/issues.

On your phone

Each app stores everything you enter in a database on the phone: Selfwise your journal, schedule, sleep and activity records; Sagax your workouts, sets, runs and plans; Sapor your meals, foods, weight entries and the photos you take of them. This data is yours, it is in the app's private storage, and the apps have no analytics, crash reporting or advertising code. Deleting the app deletes it.

  • Health Connect (Sagax, Android). With your permission Sagax reads exercise sessions from Health Connect: distance, heart rate, elevation, calories, power and cadence of runs and rides, including past ones. It stores them as runs on the phone. They reach the server only if you have an account, as part of the sync described below. Sagax writes nothing to Health Connect.
  • Camera and photos (Sapor). Sapor takes or picks meal photos with your permission and resizes them to 1280 pixels on the phone.
  • Notifications (Selfwise, Sagax). Reminders and rest timers are scheduled on the phone. No push notification service is involved.
  • Update checks. At start each app asks Expo's update server (u.expo.dev) whether a newer version of the app's code exists, and reads a small file in this repository (raw.githubusercontent.com) to learn whether a newer install is available. Those servers see your phone's IP address, the app and its version; neither receives anything you entered. On iPhone, TestFlight also sends Apple crash reports and your TestFlight feedback, which Apple shows to Cristian under Apple's TestFlight terms.

Where it is

The server runs on Cloudflare, in Cristian's Cloudflare account: the application as a Cloudflare Worker at api.selfwiselabs.com, the database on Cloudflare D1, the photos in a Cloudflare R2 bucket. Cloudflare is a US company with data centres worldwide and processes the data on Cristian's behalf under its privacy policy. All traffic between the apps and the server is encrypted (HTTPS).

  • Request logs. Cloudflare keeps logs of the requests to the server for a few days: the requesting IP address, the path, the time and the result. They do not contain your data. Cristian reads them only to look into errors and attacks.
  • Backups. Cloudflare keeps a point-in-time history of the database for 30 days. A deleted row or account can therefore still exist in that history for up to 30 days before it is gone everywhere.

If you connect Claude or ChatGPT

Nothing reaches an assistant unless you create a token on the Account screen and give it to one. Then:

  • The assistant reads and writes your data through the connector at mcp.selfwiselabs.com, which is also a Cloudflare Worker in Cristian's account. Whatever your questions make it read (your training, your journal, your meals, a meal photo) is sent to the assistant's provider: Anthropic for Claude, OpenAI for ChatGPT. From there their own privacy terms apply, including whether they keep conversations or train on them.
  • The connector keeps a copy of your rows in Cloudflare's key-value store so it can answer quickly: one copy per token, refreshed when your data changes, expiring 30 days after the last refresh. Notes the assistant leaves about the connector itself are kept for 7 days under your username.
  • A token is a key to your account. It is a secret, the ChatGPT form even puts it in the URL: anyone who has it can read your data. Revoking the token on the Account screen ends that access immediately; the copy in the key-value store expires on its own.

Who can read it

  • You, from every phone where you are signed in and through every token you created.
  • Your assistant, only if you connected one, as described above.
  • Cristian, as the operator: he has full access to the database and the photo bucket and uses it only to run the service: to fix a problem you report, to check the server for abuse, or to carry out a deletion you ask for. He does not browse other people's data.

Nobody else. The data is not sold, not shared with anyone, not used for advertising, and there are no analytics. It is handed over only if the law requires it.

Deleting your data

  • A record. Delete it in the app; the server removes its content at the next sync and your other phones follow.
  • A photo. Delete it in Sapor; the file is deleted from the server at once.
  • A phone or an assistant. Sign out on the phone, or revoke the token on the Account screen. The app's data stays on the phone until you delete the app.
  • The whole account. A Delete account button on the Account screen is being added to the three apps. Until it ships, ask Cristian (see Who runs this); he deletes the account, every row, every photo and every session within 7 days and confirms. Nothing of yours is kept afterwards except the 30-day database history Cloudflare holds.

You can also ask for a copy of everything the server holds about you; you get it as the same JSON rows the apps sync.
